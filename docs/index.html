How I work

Three habits I want every project on this site to follow.

I am still learning. These rules are partly there to stop me from talking myself into a result that the data does not really support.

01

Keep discovery and validation separate

If I found a pattern by exploring one sample, I label it that way. When possible, I freeze the candidate before I look at a new holdout.

02

Keep failed ideas in the record

If an idea fails because of a null result, bad data, hindsight bias, or a failed holdout, I would rather show the failure than quietly remove it.

03

Say what the evidence does not show

A sector-relative relationship is not the same thing as a stock-price decline. A historical holdout is not the same thing as a future prospective test.

Failed idea #001

The shorting result looked great. Then I found the problem.

I had built a rebound test inside prolonged weakness and the result looked unusually strong. The problem was that I was choosing the rebound peak with future information.

Rejected analysis

Short the rebound after extreme weakness

I threw the original result out. I rebuilt the experiment using fixed Day 5, Day 10, and Day 20 landmarks that only used information available at the time.

  1. What I saw: retrospective rebound peaks appeared to be followed by sharp declines.
  2. What was wrong: I had selected the rebound peak from the next 20 sessions, so the anchor already contained future information.
  3. What confirmed it: the apparent decline disappeared when the selected maximum occurred at the end of the search window.
  4. What I changed: I rebuilt the test around fixed prospective landmarks and kept the failed version as part of the project history.
