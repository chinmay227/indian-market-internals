The replicated finding

A larger rebound inside persistent weakness is followed by modestly worse sector-relative performance.

Among stocks still weak versus both Nifty and their sector on episode Day 20, a larger already-observed rebound from the trailing-20-session trough is associated with poorer sector-relative performance over the next 40 sessions.

The most important failed result

A strong shorting pattern disappeared when hindsight was removed.

An early experiment selected the maximum rebound inside a future 20-session window and then measured returns after that selected point. That created a mechanically favorable-looking decline.

The hindsight trap

rejected analysis

When the chosen maximum rebound occurred early in the 20-session search window, the next 10D return was mechanically negative. When the selected maximum occurred on Day 20, the apparent decline vanished.

Tempting result

Retrospective rebound peaks appeared to be followed by sharp deterioration.

02
Audit the anchor

The “entry” was chosen using future information inside the next 20 sessions.

03
Reject it

The result was treated as selection-induced rather than retained as alpha.

04
Redesign prospectively

Fixed Day 5, 10, and 20 landmarks use only information observable at the anchor.

Episode construction

Weak days were compressed into persistent states.

Consecutive sessions weak versus both Nifty and sector peers are grouped into episodes. This reduces repeated counting of essentially the same state and makes path analysis more interpretable.

Does the relationship look the same across sectors?

The locked result uses the full 400-stock holdout. This section asks a secondary diagnostic question: is that relationship broad, or concentrated in particular sectors? Sector slices are intentionally descriptive because some contain relatively few observations.

01
Rank correlation

Spearman ρ below zero means larger Day-20 rebounds tend to rank with worse subsequent sector-relative outcomes. A value near zero means little consistent ordering.

02
Rebound quartiles

Q1 contains the smallest rebounds and Q4 the largest. A clean negative pattern would generally move downward from Q1 toward Q4.

03
Sample size matters

The full holdout has 1,386 observations. A single sector can have only a few dozen, so sector-level estimates are noisier and cannot replace the locked test.

Why the sector view is secondary

Sector-specific estimates are post-validation descriptive decompositions. They help diagnose heterogeneity, but they were not individually predeclared confirmatory tests.

Methodology

Designed to make weak results disappear when they should.

01

Relative returns

Stocks are compared with Nifty 50 and a leave-one-out equal-weight sector peer benchmark.

02

Continuous weakness features

Magnitude, underperformance days, concentration, effective negative days, and evenness are kept separate rather than collapsed prematurely.

03

Episodes

Consecutive weak-both sessions form one episode; no minimum duration and no gap stitching are imposed in the primary definition.

04

Prospective anchors

Day-20 predictor history ends on the anchor. Every forward outcome starts strictly at t+1.

05

Dependence control

Primary episode starts for a stock are spaced by more than 60 market sessions.

06

Locked holdout

The 400-stock complement, primary outcome, and success criteria were frozen before validation returns were inspected.

Known limitation: the historical universe uses current Nifty 500 membership. The study therefore has current-membership / survivorship bias and is not a historical constituent reconstruction.
