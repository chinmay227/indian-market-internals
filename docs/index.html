Research standards

Three rules guide the work on this site.

The purpose is not to maximize the number of positive findings. The purpose is to make weak findings harder to keep.

01

Keep discovery and validation separate

Patterns found through exploration are labeled as exploratory. When possible, the candidate is frozen before a new holdout is inspected.

02

Keep failed ideas in the record

Null results, data problems, failed holdouts, and methodological errors remain visible because they are part of the research process.

03

Say what the evidence does not show

A sector-relative relationship is not the same thing as a stock-price decline. A historical holdout is not the same thing as a future prospective test.

Failed idea #001

A promising shorting result turned out to depend on hindsight.

An early rebound test inside prolonged weakness looked strong enough to resemble a potentially useful shorting strategy. The result was later rejected because the rebound peak had been selected using future information.

Rejected analysis

Short the rebound after extreme weakness

The original result was removed from the candidate set. A fixed Day-20 redesign produced a much smaller sector-relative relationship.

  1. Initial result: retrospective rebound peaks appeared to be followed by sharp declines.
  2. Problem: the rebound peak was selected from the next 20 sessions, so the historical anchor already contained future information.
  3. Audit result: the apparent decline disappeared when the selected maximum occurred at the end of the search window.
  4. Redesign: fixed Day 5, Day 10, and Day 20 landmarks used only information available at the time.
