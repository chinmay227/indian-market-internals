Can an LLM tell when a market price deserves less trust?

I am testing whether LLM agents update differently when the same numerical prediction-market price comes from an informative setting versus one where a strategic participant may have distorted it.

Experimental designPilot in progressNo empirical claim yet

Why I am interested in this

Prediction markets are attractive because a price can summarize dispersed information. But that summary is only useful if the mechanism that produced the price is informative. I want to know whether an LLM reasons about that distinction, or whether it tends to treat “the market price” as authoritative regardless of where the price came from.

The core test

I can show two agents the same numerical market price while changing what they know about its provenance. If one price comes from informed trading and the other may have been moved by a manipulator, a coherent posterior should not necessarily weight those prices equally.

The question is not whether the model can copy a market probability. The question is whether it understands when the market probability itself should be discounted.

Why I am starting with synthetic events

Real prediction markets do not reveal the true information set behind a price move. In a synthetic environment I can control the prior, private signals, signal quality, public price, source credibility, and manipulator information. That gives me a known data-generating process and a Bayesian benchmark to compare the model against.

What this gives me

I can vary provenance while holding the observed market price fixed. That makes it possible to ask whether the model responds to the source of the information rather than merely reacting to the number on screen.

What I plan to measure

The main outcome is how much weight the model places on the public price under different source conditions. A model that reacts almost identically to a well-informed price and a strategically distorted price would fail the mechanism test even if some of its final probabilities happen to be accurate.

Where the project stands

The experimental structure and agent workflow are defined. The real-model pilot is still in progress, so I am not presenting a result yet. I am also keeping the Kalshi and Polymarket cross-venue discrepancy work as a separate research strand rather than mixing two different questions into one paper.

What would change my mind?

If LLM responses do not move systematically with source credibility, or if any apparent effect disappears across models and prompt variants, then the provenance mechanism is not supported. That would still be a useful result.
