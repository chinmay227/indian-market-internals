Price Provenance and Belief Updating in Prediction Markets

Working question: do LLM agents distinguish informative price movements from strategically distorted prices, or do they treat “the market price” as equally credible regardless of how it was generated?

Experimental designPilot in progressNo empirical claim yet

Research question

The project tests source-sensitive probability updating. If two agents observe the same numerical market price but receive different information about its provenance, should their posterior beliefs differ — and do LLMs behave accordingly?

The central distinction is not simply “market price versus private belief.” It is whether the observed price is informative because it aggregates useful information, or distorted because a strategic participant can move it.

Experimental design

The first version is deliberately local-first and synthetic. Binary events are generated with known priors, likelihoods, private signals, signal reliability, public-price provenance, and manipulator information or budget. Because the data-generating process is known, a normative Bayesian posterior can be computed and used as a benchmark.

Why synthetic first?

Real prediction markets do not reveal the true information set or causal provenance of a price move. Synthetic events let the experiment vary provenance while holding the observed price fixed and know what a coherent posterior should look like.

Primary test

Does the weight an LLM assigns to the public market price track the disclosed informativeness of that price source? A model that reacts identically to a well-informed price and a strategically manipulated price would fail the mechanism test even if its final probability occasionally happens to be accurate.

Current status

The research design, agent-simulation concepts, and data workflow are defined. Real-model execution and the first controlled pilot remain in progress. Cross-venue Kalshi–Polymarket discrepancy work is being kept as a separate research strand rather than mixed into the core provenance experiment.

What would count as a useful result?

Not simply higher trading P&L. The stronger evidence would be systematic, directionally correct changes in probability weighting as source credibility changes, measured against the known normative benchmark.
