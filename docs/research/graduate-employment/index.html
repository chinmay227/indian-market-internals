Young-Graduate Employment Across Indian States

Across major Indian states, how did young graduates’ unemployment and access to secure salaried employment change from PLFS 2017–18 through 2023–24, and were within-state changes in those two margins related?

Data validationDescriptive panel studyNot a causal design

Why this question?

Graduate unemployment is often discussed as a single headline rate. This project separates two margins: whether young graduates can enter employment at all, and whether employed graduates obtain regular salaried work with indicators of employment security.

Planned sample and outcomes

The target population is young adults aged 20–29. The graduate group uses the PLFS higher-education categories corresponding to graduate and postgraduate education, with a lower-education comparison group used for context.

Primary outcomes

Unemployment is measured among the labour force. Regular-salaried access is measured among the employed. Contract, paid-leave, and social-security outcomes are evaluated within regular salaried employment rather than mixed into the unemployment denominator.

Panel structure

The aim is a consistent seven-round state-by-year panel from 2017–18 to 2023–24 using annual survey weights, pooled weighted ratios, official-table validation, and design-consistent uncertainty where feasible.

Contribution

The broad fact of elevated graduate unemployment is already known. The intended contribution is narrower: a consistent state-year view of unemployment and job quality, with an explicit distinction between within-state change over time and persistent differences between states.

Current status

Official documentation, variable inventories, and validation targets have been assembled. The current work is data harmonization and validation across annual PLFS rounds before substantive results are reported.
