What does persistent stock weakness actually look like?

The project starts from a familiar momentum intuition: if a stock keeps underperforming both the market and its own sector, perhaps the path of that weakness contains information about what happens next. The strongest early result was invalid, the surviving holdout relationship is small, and two intuitive rebound mechanisms have now failed to explain it.

What survived

A small relationship held up in the 400-stock holdout.

Among stocks whose weakness episode was still running on Day 20, larger rebounds from the recent stock-price low tended to be followed by slightly worse performance versus sector peers over the next 40 market sessions. The relationship was much smaller in validation than it looked in discovery.

The setup in plain English

A weakness episode is a run of consecutive sessions in which a stock is weak relative to both Nifty 50 and its sector. If that episode survives to Day 20, the rebound measures how far the stock price has bounced from its lowest price in the previous 20 sessions. Future performance is then measured against sector peers over the next 40 sessions.

Step 1Find persistent weakness

The stock remains weak versus both Nifty and its sector through Day 20.

Step 2Measure the rebound

Rebound size is the distance from the lowest stock price in the trailing 20 sessions to the Day-20 price.

Step 3Watch the next 40 sessions

Negative sector-relative return means the stock lagged its sector over the following 40 market sessions.

One statistic appears repeatedly below: Spearman ρ. It is a rank correlation. A negative value means larger rebounds tend to line up with worse later outcomes. It is not a percentage return. A value of −0.061 does not mean the stock falls 6.1%.

Phase 12A · mechanism tests

Two intuitive explanations did not hold up.

Phase 12A asked why some rebounds inside persistent weakness appear to be followed by continued sector-relative lag. Two mechanisms were written down before the results were viewed: peer confirmation and rebound concentration. Neither was supported strongly enough to carry forward as a prospective mechanism.

A useful change in perspective

The original Phase 10 sample contained 1,386 independent Day-20 observations. Rebound quality only makes sense when a positive rebound actually occurred before Day 20, leaving 1,128 observations across 345 stocks. Inside that positive-rebound subset, the original rebound-to-future-sector relationship weakened from ρ = −0.061 to about ρ = −0.034. That suggests the surviving Phase 10 result is not simply a story about “large rebounds being bad.”

Conclusion

One-day concentration does not appear to explain why the larger-rebound observations later lag their sectors.

An exploratory clue, not a new confirmed result: rebound concentration was very strongly related to how recently the stock had made its trough. Concentration versus rebound-window length had Spearman ρ ≈ −0.85. A post-result diagnostic suggested trough age carried more information about whether the weakness episode itself remained active than concentration did. Because this was noticed after viewing Phase 12A, it is being kept exploratory rather than promoted to a validated finding.

How I read Phase 12A: the two intuitive price-path explanations failed, which is useful. The next mechanism test moves outside the stock-price path and asks whether derivatives positioning contains information that price-only features missed.

The failed test

A promising result turned out to be created by hindsight.

The early experiment searched forward for the largest rebound inside a 20-session window and then measured returns after that selected peak. That historical “entry” could only be known after seeing the future path.

How the look-ahead problem revealed itself

Cases are grouped by where the retrospectively selected maximum rebound occurred inside the future 20-session search window.

rejected analysis
When the chosen maximum occurred early, the subsequent return looked extremely negative. When the chosen maximum occurred on Day 20, the apparent deterioration disappeared.
What convinced meThe early selected peaks mechanically left room for prices to move down from a future maximum.
What changed nextThe result was rejected and the experiment moved to fixed landmarks that could have been known at the time.

04
The experiment was rebuilt

Day 5, Day 10, and Day 20 landmarks use only information available by the landmark date.

Episode definition

Consecutive weak days are grouped into episodes to avoid repeated counting.

A weakness episode continues while the stock remains weak relative to both Nifty and its sector. Once that condition breaks, the episode ends.

How long do weakness episodes last?

9,656 discovery episodes

Many episodes are short. The episode framework prevents several adjacent weak days from being treated as several independent experiments.

Method

The design reduces hindsight and repeated counting.

The full notebook trail and working paper are in the repository. The points below are the design choices that matter most for interpreting the result.

01

Relative benchmarks

Each stock is compared with Nifty 50 and a leave-one-out equal-weight sector peer benchmark.

02

Separate features

Magnitude, persistence, concentration, and related features are not collapsed into one optimized score.

03

Episodes

Consecutive weak-both sessions form one episode instead of repeated stock-day observations.

04

Prospective anchors

Predictor history ends at the fixed landmark; future outcomes begin strictly at t+1.

05

Dependence control

Primary same-stock episode starts are spaced by more than 60 market sessions.

06

Locked holdout

The Day-20 predictor, 40-session sector-relative outcome, holdout universe, and pass criteria were frozen before validation.

Known limitation: historical analysis uses current Nifty 500 membership, creating current-membership / survivorship bias.
Next evidence

Phase 12B moves from price shape to derivatives positioning.

The next predeclared mechanism uses aggregate open interest across all active NSE single-stock futures expiries. The question is whether a positive rebound accompanied by increasing outstanding futures positions behaves differently from a rebound that occurs while aggregate futures open interest is falling. Price-up plus OI-down is treated only as a position-reduction proxy, not proof of short covering.
