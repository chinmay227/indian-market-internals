I wanted to know what persistent stock weakness actually looks like.

I started with a fairly intuitive idea: if a stock keeps underperforming both the market and its own sector, maybe that weakness has a path that tells us something about what happens next. Most of the obvious versions of that idea were much weaker than I expected.

What survived

I found a small relationship that held up in the 400-stock holdout.

Among stocks whose weakness episode was still running on Day 20, larger rebounds from the recent stock-price low tended to be followed by slightly worse performance versus sector peers over the next 40 market sessions. The relationship was much smaller in validation than it looked in discovery.

The setup in plain English

I am not asking whether a stock is simply down. I first define a weakness episode as consecutive sessions in which the stock is weak relative to both Nifty 50 and its sector. If that episode survives to Day 20, I measure how far the stock price has bounced from its lowest price in the previous 20 sessions. I then ask how the stock performs versus sector peers over the next 40 sessions.

Step 1Find persistent weakness

The stock remains weak versus both Nifty and its sector through Day 20 of the episode.

Step 2Measure the rebound

On Day 20, I measure how far the stock price is above its lowest price from the trailing 20 sessions.

Step 3Watch the next 40 sessions

I compare the stock with its sector peers. Negative sector-relative return means the stock lagged its sector.

One statistic appears repeatedly below: Spearman ρ. It is a rank correlation. A negative value means larger rebounds tend to line up with worse later outcomes. It is not a percentage return. A value of −0.061 does not mean the stock falls 6.1%.

Does the negative sign survive different ways of resampling the data?

Rows from the same stock, quarter, or sector are not fully independent. I therefore resampled the validation data in clusters rather than pretending every observation was unrelated.

2,000 bootstrap draws
The dot is the typical resampled rank correlation. The horizontal line is the 95% interval. The vertical zero line means no rank relationship. If an interval crosses zero, the sign is less stable under that clustering choice.
How I read itAll three intervals remain just below zero. The calendar-quarter interval nearly touches zero, so I treat the result as modest rather than strong.
Why I show all threeThe result should not depend on one convenient definition of independence. Stock, time, and sector clustering stress different forms of dependence.
The main limit of the result: the effect is mainly relative to sector peers. It does not show that a stock with a larger rebound is likely to fall in absolute price.

What exactly underperformed after a larger rebound?

I reran the same ranking against three future outcomes to see whether the result was really about the stock falling, lagging Nifty, or lagging its own sector.

validation sample
Only the sector-relative outcome has the same modest negative relationship. Nifty-relative and absolute-stock outcomes are close to zero.
How I read itThe candidate is better described as a sector-relative lag relationship than a directional short signal.
What the evidence does not showIt does not show that the stock itself will necessarily fall, and it does not establish trading profitability after costs.
The failed test

I nearly kept a result that was created by hindsight.

I originally searched forward for the largest rebound inside a 20-session window and then measured returns after that selected peak. That sounds reasonable until you notice that the historical “entry” can only be known after seeing the future path.

How the look-ahead problem revealed itself

I grouped cases by where the retrospectively selected maximum rebound happened inside the future 20-session search window.

rejected analysis
When the chosen maximum occurred early, the subsequent return looked extremely negative. When the chosen maximum occurred on Day 20, there was no future window left inside the selection process and the apparent decline disappeared.
What convinced meThe early selected peaks mechanically left room for prices to move down from a future maximum. The Day-20 group was roughly neutral afterward.
What I changedI threw this result out and moved to fixed landmarks that could actually have been known at the time.

How I define an episode

I group consecutive weak days so I do not count the same state over and over.

A weakness episode continues while the stock remains weak relative to both Nifty and its sector. Once that condition breaks, the episode ends.

Most weakness episodes are short

The discovery sample produced 9,656 episodes. The duration distribution is heavily concentrated in the first few sessions.

9,656 episodes
The x-axis groups episode length in market sessions. The y-axis counts distinct episodes rather than individual weak stock-days.
How I read itAbout 31% of episodes last only one session, and the median episode lasts three sessions. Reaching Day 20 is therefore a much more persistent state than the typical episode.
Why this mattersThe Day-20 sample is not “all weak stocks.” It is the subset that has stayed weak versus both benchmarks for a relatively long time.

Method

These are the choices I made to reduce hindsight and repeated counting.

The full notebook trail and working paper are in the repository. This section is the short version of the design decisions that matter most for interpreting the result.

01

Relative returns

I compare each stock with Nifty 50 and with a leave-one-out equal-weight sector peer benchmark.

02

Weakness features stay separate

I do not collapse magnitude, persistence, concentration, and related features into one optimized score at this stage.

03

Consecutive days form one episode

This reduces repeated counting of what is essentially the same prolonged weak state.

04

Fixed landmarks

The Day-20 predictor uses information available by Day 20. Every forward outcome starts at the next session.

05

Spacing repeated episodes

Primary same-stock episode starts are separated by more than 60 market sessions.

06

Locked holdout

I froze the Day-20 predictor, 40-session sector-relative outcome, holdout universe, and pass criteria before looking at the validation result.

Known limitation: the historical universe uses current Nifty 500 membership rather than a reconstructed historical constituent universe. That creates current-membership and survivorship bias.
Future-time test

I have stopped changing the historical candidate.

The historical research cutoff is 21 August 2026. New Day-20 episode landmarks after that date will be evaluated with the same rebound measure and the same next-40-session sector-relative outcome. I will not use new historical tuning to redefine the candidate while waiting.
