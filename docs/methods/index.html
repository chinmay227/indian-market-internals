Research standards

Methods

A plain-English and technical reference for the design choices used across the site.

Discovery ≠ validation

Patterns discovered after looking at a dataset are exploratory. A stronger test freezes the relevant specification before inspecting a new holdout.

Failures stay visible

Nulls and broken designs are not silently deleted. They become part of the audit trail and can motivate redesign.

Effect shrinkage is reported

If a correlation falls from −0.188 in discovery to −0.061 in validation, the smaller estimate is not hidden.

Limitations are part of the result

Survivorship, incomplete historical membership, measurement constraints, and dependence are disclosed where they matter.

Spearman ρ

A rank-based correlation. Negative ρ means higher values of one variable tend to rank with lower values of the other; it does not mean a 1% change in X causes a fixed percentage change in Y.

Holdout sample

Data not used to discover or tune the candidate. A holdout reduces the chance that a pattern exists only because the researcher kept adjusting the specification to one sample.

Prospective test

A test evaluated only on observations that occur after the rule is frozen. This is stronger than a cross-sectional holdout over the same historical period.

Look-ahead bias

Using information that would not have been known at the historical decision point. It can create apparently powerful backtests that could not have been executed in real time.

Cluster bootstrap

A resampling procedure that keeps related observations together — for example by stock, quarter, or sector — so uncertainty reflects dependence rather than pretending every row is independent.

Survivorship bias

Studying only entities that survived into a later universe can make history look cleaner than it really was. The equity-weakness project explicitly documents current-membership bias.
