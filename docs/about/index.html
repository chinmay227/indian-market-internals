I studied Computer Information Systems and data analytics at Georgia State University and previously worked in derivatives-clearing operations at Morgan Stanley. I did not come into this through a traditional economics or quantitative-finance degree, which is part of why I am deliberate about showing the methods and limitations behind the work.

The common thread in what I enjoy is fairly simple: I like taking a vague question, deciding what can actually be measured, building a test that can fail, and then figuring out what to do when the evidence does not match the original story.

What I am interested in

  • Indian equity research, market structure, and empirical asset-pricing questions;
  • quantitative trading research without hiding failed backtests;
  • prediction markets, information aggregation, and AI decision-making;
  • labour economics and Indian public microdata such as PLFS;
  • optimization, research design, and reproducible analytical workflows.

Why I keep failed ideas on the site

I do not want this to become a page where every project ends with a clean positive finding. If I make a methodological mistake, if a holdout fails, or if a plausible idea turns out to be economically trivial, that belongs in the research record too.

What this site is not

It is not a signal-selling service or a claim that every research question produces alpha. Company-specific equity work will be separated from quantitative research, and I will state assumptions, uncertainty, and relevant conflicts where they matter.
